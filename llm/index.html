How to Train Your LLM

The one-line story of language model training is well known: predict the next token, update the weights, repeat over the internet. The story is true, and it explains very little. It will not tell you why the loss curve bends where it does, or what a data mixture decision costs, or what alignment training takes out of a model.

That gap used to be a specialist's problem. It is not anymore. Whether a model can be trusted with a task, where it will fail, and what it would take to fix it are questions whose answers get set during training, and nearly everyone now reasoning about those questions, including many people building on these models, is reasoning from the outside.

The way to get inside is to train one. That is what this series is: a small model, built from scratch in this browser tab, through the same stages as a frontier run. You assemble its data, train its tokenizer, pretrain it, find its scaling law, and tune it into an assistant. It starts with the job the model has to do.
